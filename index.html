Before you start

What you can and can't remove

Being straight about this up front saves you from chasing things that will never come down.

Three things are permanently out of reach

CategoryWhy it stays
Public recordsProperty deeds, voter registration, court filings, marriage and divorce records, business registrations, professional licences. Public by law. Brokers that republish them can be stopped; the underlying record cannot.
Data already soldAnything a broker sold before you opted out now lives in someone else's database. Deletion is forward-looking only.
Archives and leaksThe Internet Archive, screenshots, scraped copies, and breach datasets circulating in criminal markets.

What you actually achieve: you make yourself expensive to look up. Almost every casual search — a nosy colleague, a scam caller, someone with a grudge and no budget — stops at the free tier of people-search sites. Clearing those, plus the wholesalers feeding them, removes the large majority of real-world exposure.

This decays. Brokers re-scrape the same public sources. Expect some data back within 3–12 months. Everything here needs a repeat cycle — which is the strongest argument for eventually paying someone to do it.

Step 1

Foundations

Freezes, monitoring, and the two accounts you need to create before touching any opt-out form.

Set up a throwaway email first. Many brokers add your opt-out address to their marketing lists. Never use your primary email on these forms, and use a Google Voice number wherever phone verification is required.

Step 2

People-search sites

The main event. These expose your home address, phone, age, prior addresses, and relatives to anyone who types your name.

Search yourself on each site first and paste the profile URL into the note field — most opt-outs require it. Watch for duplicate listings: old addresses and name variants often produce two or three profiles per site, and removing one leaves the others live.

Never pay a people-search site to remove you, and decline every "premium removal" upsell. Free opt-out is legally required. Never send a photo of your ID to one of these sites.

Roughly 70 more sites exist below this tier. Maintained lists with current links, better than any static table: 104-site opt-out index · Security Hero step-by-step · C.A. Goldberg (victims' rights firm) · EasyOptOuts 200+ index.

Step 3

Upstream aggregators

The wholesalers. Most people-search sites are retail outlets for this data — suppressing here slows how fast your removed listings come back.

Expect these to be slower and more bureaucratic than the retail sites; a few want a mailed form with ID. Worth the friction. If one blows the 45-day statutory deadline, that's an enforceable violation — file with your state Attorney General.

Step 4

Accounts and social

Settings you own outright, plus the dormant accounts quietly holding your data until their next breach.

Leverage

Use your legal rights

Statutory rights most people never invoke. They turn a polite request into a legal obligation with a deadline.

In California? Do this before anything else. DROP — the Delete Request and Opt-out Platform has been live since 1 January 2026 under the Delete Act. Create one profile, verify residency, submit one request — and it reaches all 575+ data brokers registered in California. Brokers must start honouring requests from 1 August 2026 and check the platform every 45 days. Nothing else in the country comes close. Submit here before paying for any removal service — it may cover most of what you'd be buying.

Everywhere else

About 20 states now have comprehensive privacy laws granting a right to delete. As of 1 January 2026, twelve states legally require businesses to honour Global Privacy Control signals: California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Maryland, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Oregon and Texas.

Install Global Privacy Control — it's built into Brave, the DuckDuckGo browser, and Firefox (Settings → Privacy → "Tell websites not to sell or share my data"). In those twelve states it's a legally binding opt-out on every site you visit, automatically, forever.

The standard response window is 45 days, extendable once by another 45.

Request template

For brokers with no self-service form. Send it from your throwaway address.

Subject: Consumer Request to Delete and Opt Out of Sale — [YOUR FULL NAME]

To Whom It May Concern:

I am a resident of [STATE]. Pursuant to [CCPA/CPRA — or your state's statute],
I am exercising my rights to:

  1. Delete all personal information you have collected about me;
  2. Opt out of the sale and sharing of my personal information;
  3. Limit the use and disclosure of my sensitive personal information;
  4. Direct all service providers and third parties to whom you have sold or
     disclosed my information to do the same.

Identifying information: [full name, any prior or variant names, current
address, prior addresses, date of birth, phone, email]

Please confirm completion in writing and identify all categories of third
parties to whom my information was sold or disclosed in the preceding
12 months.

I expect a response within 45 days as required by law.

[NAME]

The part everyone skips

Upkeep

Doing this once, thoroughly, and never again is the single most common failure. Set the reminders now.

CadenceTask
Weekly, first monthCheck the throwaway inbox for confirmation links. Most opt-outs die because a verification email went unclicked.
MonthlySearch your name, name + city, name + employer, your phone number, and your email. Log new hits.
QuarterlyRe-run the tier-one opt-outs. Re-check any service dashboard. Re-check Have I Been Pwned.
AnnuallyFull sweep — aggregators, confirm freezes are still active, review social settings after policy changes.
After any moveEverything resets. A new address enters public records and propagates within weeks. Run the whole list again.

Stop the resupply

These habits matter more over a year than any single opt-out.

AreaPractice
EmailUse aliases for new signups — a real aliasing service (SimpleLogin, Fastmail, Apple Hide My Email, DuckDuckGo Email Protection), not Gmail's +tag, which brokers strip trivially. Keep three tiers: financial/government (never given out), real people, everything else.
PhoneA Google Voice or MySudo number for retail, loyalty schemes, appointments, and deliveries. Your real mobile goes to banks, doctors, and people who know you. This is the biggest single reducer of spam calls, because brokers correlate on phone number more than any other field.
AddressA PO box, UPS Store box, or virtual mailbox for anything not legally required to be your residence. Property records will still show your home; everything downstream doesn't have to.
BrowserBrave or Firefox with GPC enabled, plus uBlock Origin. Stops new collection rather than removing old data — which over a year matters more.

If someone specific is looking for you, the order changes: address suppression comes before anything cosmetic, and you shouldn't wait out the 45-day statutory windows. Around 40 states run an Address Confidentiality Program for survivors of domestic violence, stalking, sexual assault or trafficking — it gives you a legal substitute address for voter registration, licences, school forms and court filings, and seals the underlying public record, which is otherwise the one layer no opt-out can reach. Administered through your Secretary of State or Attorney General's office. If you qualify, it's worth more than everything else on this page combined. The Safety Net project at NNEDV has guidance written for exactly this threat model.
